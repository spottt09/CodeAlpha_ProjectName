# Social Engineering: A Focus on Phishing
## 1. Introduction

Social engineering is a cyberattack technique that manipulates people into revealing confidential information, granting unauthorized access, or performing actions that compromise security.

Instead of attacking systems directly, attackers exploit human trust, curiosity, fear, and urgency.

## 2. What Is Phishing?

Phishing is one of the most common forms of social engineering. It involves sending fake emails, messages, or websites that impersonate trusted organizations or individuals to steal sensitive information.

**Common targets include**:

- Login credentials and passwords
- Banking and financial information
- Personal information
- One-time passwords (OTPs)
## 3. Common Types of Phishing
- **Email Phishing**: Fake emails designed to steal information.
- **Spear Phishing**: Targeted phishing attacks against specific individuals.
- **Smishing**: Phishing through SMS or text messages.
- **Vishing**: Phishing through phone calls.
- **Clone Phishing**: Copying legitimate messages and replacing links or attachments with malicious ones.
## 4. How Phishing Works
1. The attacker creates a fake email, message, or website.
2. The victim receives a convincing message.
3. The message encourages the victim to click a link or open an attachment.
4. The victim unknowingly shares sensitive information or downloads malicious software.
5. The attacker uses the stolen information for unauthorized access or fraud.
## 5. Warning Signs of Phishing
- Suspicious or unfamiliar sender addresses.
- Unexpected links or attachments.
- Urgent messages demanding immediate action.
- Spelling and grammatical mistakes.
- Requests for passwords, OTPs, or personal information.
- Fake websites with unusual domain names.
## 6. How to Prevent Phishing
- Always verify the sender before responding.
- Avoid clicking suspicious links or attachments.
- Check website URLs carefully.
- Never share passwords or OTPs.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Use updated antivirus and security software.
- Report suspicious messages to your IT or security team.
## 7. Conclusion

Social engineering demonstrates that humans can be the weakest link in cybersecurity. Phishing remains a major threat because it exploits trust rather than technical vulnerabilities.

**Think Before You Click — Stay Alert, Stay Secure!**
